BOOK THREE · PIP & WAFFLES

Waffles and Pip and the Missing Carrot Mystery

A carrot. A mystery. A very muddy detective.

Waffles the white rabbit had a problem.

A very serious problem.

A very orange problem.

His carrot was gone.

Waffles stared at the empty patch of dirt.

“No.”

Pip the little blue bird landed beside him.

“What happened?”

Waffles pointed dramatically.

“My carrot.”

Pip looked at the dirt.

“What about it?”

“It has vanished.”

Pip blinked.

“You ate it.”

Waffles gasped.

“I would remember eating a carrot.”

Pip stared at him.

“Would you?”

Waffles thought about it.

“…Probably.”

Pip hopped closer to the patch.

“Maybe someone took it.”

Waffles’ ears shot straight up.

“A CARROT THIEF.”

Pip sighed.

“I did not say thief.”

“A masked carrot bandit!”

“I definitely did not say masked.”

Waffles began inspecting the ground.

He looked under a leaf.

No carrot.

2 / 16

He looked behind a rock.

No carrot.

He looked inside an old flowerpot.

A beetle looked back at him.

“Excuse me,” said Waffles.

The beetle crawled away.

“Suspicious,” whispered Waffles.

“The beetle did not steal your carrot,” said Pip.

“That is exactly what the beetle wants you to think.”

3 / 16

Pip flapped onto a fence post.

“Do you see any clues?”

Waffles put his nose very close to the ground.

He sniffed.

He sniffed again.

Then—

“AH-CHOO!”

A puff of dirt flew straight onto Pip.

Pip froze.

Waffles froze.

Pip slowly looked down at her dusty feathers.

“Waffles.”

“Yes?”

“You sneezed dirt on me.”

“It was evidence.”

“It was dirt.”

4 / 16

“Evidence dirt.”

Pip shook herself clean.

“Keep looking.”

Waffles found tiny tracks near the garden.

He gasped.

“PIP!”

“What?”

“FOOTPRINTS!”

Pip swooped down.

She studied them carefully.

“They’re very small.”

Waffles nodded.

“Small criminal.”

“They look like mouse tracks.”

Waffles whispered, “Mouse criminal.”

They followed the tracks past the garden.

5 / 16

Across the path.

Under the fence.

Around a stump.

Then the tracks disappeared.

Waffles stared at the ground.

“They vanished.”

“Maybe the mouse climbed something,” said Pip.

Waffles looked up.

A squirrel sat on a branch eating a nut.

Waffles narrowed his eyes.

The squirrel narrowed his eyes.

Waffles narrowed his eyes more.

The squirrel went back to eating.

“Suspicious,” said Waffles.

“You think everyone is suspicious.”

“That is because everyone keeps behaving suspiciously.”

6 / 16

Pip flew around the tree.

“No carrot up here.”

Waffles sniffed the air.

His nose twitched.

Then twitched again.

“Wait.”

“What?”

“I smell something.”

“Carrot?”

“No.”

Pip waited.

Waffles sniffed again.

“Pie.”

Pip groaned.

“Stay focused.”

“But what if the carrot is in the pie?”

7 / 16

“It is not.”

“How do we know?”

“Because we are not investigating pie.”

Waffles looked disappointed.

They continued down the path.

8 / 16

Then Pip spotted something orange near a bush.

“There!”

Waffles bolted forward.

“My carrot!”

He pounced.

He grabbed the orange object.

He pulled.

He pulled harder.

He fell backward.

PLOP!

Pip landed beside him.

Waffles held up…

a flower.

He stared at it.

“This is not my carrot.”

“No.”

9 / 16

“It tricked me.”

“It is a flower.”

“A very sneaky flower.”

They kept searching.

10 / 16

Soon they found a hole under a log.

Waffles crouched low.

Pip whispered, “What are you doing?”

“Detective creeping.”

“You look like you’re trying not to sneeze.”

“I am being mysterious.”

A tiny mouse poked her head out of the hole.

Waffles gasped.

“CARROT THIEF!”

The mouse squeaked.

Pip smacked Waffles gently with one wing.

“Waffles!”

“What?”

“You cannot yell ‘carrot thief’ at everyone.”

The mouse frowned.

“I did not take your carrot.”

11 / 16

Waffles leaned closer.

“Do you know anything about the disappearance?”

The mouse thought for a moment.

“I saw something orange rolling down the hill.”

Waffles’ eyes widened.

“ROLLING?”

The mouse nodded.

“Fast.”

Waffles turned to Pip.

“My carrot escaped.”

Pip stared at him.

“Carrots do not escape.”

“This one apparently did.”

They raced toward the hill.

Waffles hopped.

Pip flew.

The mouse followed because now she wanted to know what happened too.

At the bottom of the hill was a muddy puddle.

And sticking out of the mud was—

AN ORANGE TOP.

“My carrot!”

Waffles leaped into the puddle.

SPLASH!

Mud flew everywhere.

Pip got covered again.

The mouse got covered too.

Waffles pulled.

Nothing.

He pulled harder.

Nothing.

Pip grabbed the green leaves with her beak.

The mouse grabbed a leaf.

“Ready?” said Pip.

“READY!”

They pulled.

They tugged.

They grunted.

Then—

POP!

Out came…

a very muddy carrot.

Waffles hugged it.

“My beautiful carrot!”

Pip looked at him.

“You are hugging mud.”

“It is carrot-shaped mud.”

The mouse laughed.

Pip laughed.

Waffles looked at the carrot.

Then at Pip.

Then at the mouse.

“I suppose there is only one thing left to do.”

Pip smiled.

“Wash it?”

“No.”

“Eat it?”

“No.”

“What then?”

Waffles pointed dramatically toward the garden.

“We must find out who pushed it down the hill.”

Pip groaned.

The mouse laughed harder.

Waffles took one bite of the muddy carrot.

Crunch.

Pip stared at him.

“You were going to wash that.”

Waffles chewed.

“I changed my mind.”

“You are eating dirt.”

“Evidence dirt.”

And off they went—

one very muddy white rabbit,

one very muddy blue bird,

one very amused mouse,

and one carrot mystery that was probably never a mystery at all.

The End
